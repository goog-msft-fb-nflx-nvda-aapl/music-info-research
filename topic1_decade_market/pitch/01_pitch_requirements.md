# Pitch requirements (from `final_proj_pitch.pdf`, 2026 edition, Yi-Hsuan Yang)
What is a project pitch: see NSF SBIR "project pitch" and the pyramid principle (links on the slide).
1. **Technology innovation** — technical innovation that is the focus; its origin (motivation); why it is a good final project for the course.
2. **Technical objectives and challenges** — why it is hard/non-trivial; how you plan to approach it; your goals.
3. **Feasibility analysis** — why it is not too hard; familiarity with the topic; major reference papers; existing datasets; related codebase; GPU compute needed and available; milestones with tangible intermediate results; quick pilot studies / prototypes.
4. **The team** — previous ML experience, music-related background, which lab(s).
Logistics: presentation in W12, **5 minutes per team**, TA's laptop is used, slides must reach the TAs **at least one day earlier**. Hint link: github.com/jbhuang0604/awesome-tips.

# Topic text (from `final_project_ideas.pdf`, topic 1)
"Released decade/market classification (extensions of HW1). Research question: does a cover sound like the era of the original song, or the era in which the cover was released? Build pairs of different recordings of the same song from Discogs-VI (ISMIR'24) — select versions released in different decades; verify release years and exclude reissues. Examine how era cues change between versions — compare predictions from the full mix, vocals and accompaniment; relate the predicted era shift to the actual release-year gap and listen to contrasting examples. Possible extension: use the identified cues to guide era-controlled cover song generation." Teams of 3–4; pitch W12; final presentation W16; report W16+2.
