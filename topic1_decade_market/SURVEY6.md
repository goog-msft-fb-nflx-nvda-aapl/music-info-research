## 1. Is it style transfer? (course material)
Lecture 11b (Chih-Pin Tan, "Music Adaptation: Arrangement & Cover Generation") formulates cover song generation as **content-preserving style transfer**: content = melody and chords (and structure), style = everything else (genre, instrumentation, arrangement, rhythm and timbre expression). Its definition of an adaptation explicitly includes "translating a song into another language". It lists SongEcho (ACE-Step based, text-guided), PiCoGen, Pop2Piano, BOSSA, AccoMontage as the audio and symbolic cover systems, and states that evaluation is "a mess": style is ill-defined and metrics from general music generation do not fit covers. Our task therefore fits: the control plane (era, market) is the **style** variable, the content is melody + chords + (lyric meaning). It differs from the usual formulation in two ways: (1) the style variable is a categorical-ordinal metadata coordinate (release era, release market) instead of a genre label or a reference clip; (2) the market change involves the **lyrics**, which are content in the usual formulation but must change with the style here.

## 3. Findings from the survey
1. **No paper found combines the three requirements together**: (a) change of lyrics language, (b) change of the accompaniment style by a metadata coordinate, (c) melody and singer preserved. Closest: PolySinger (a + c, one language pair, vocal only, no accompaniment re-styling), SongEcho and MPEcho (c + text style prompt, lyrics given, no translation, no market/era coordinate). This is a search result, not a proof of absence; the survey must be repeated after the next round of experiments.
2. **Translation must be singable**: published work treats syllable count, rhythm alignment, rhyme and (for tonal languages) tone as explicit constraints (GagaST decoding constraints; Lyra rhythm constraints; MAVL syllable-constrained model). Our current route translates text and lets the generator fit it; it has no such constraint and no measurement of rhythm fit. This is a gap in our market axis and the first thing to measure (see TODO).
3. **How single-market work does it**: the cascaded systems (PolySinger) transcribe, align, translate, then synthesize on the extracted melody; the translation step is the weak link (a thesis on the same task reported poor singability of the translated lyrics [search, AAU project page]).
4. **Real-world translation keeps the music; the market change of the music itself (S5) is not covered by the lyric work**. The cultural-adaptation work (Mehta et al.) treats genre traditions, not release markets of pop recordings; our dataset B has only labels (market, 1980s) and the market probes read the vocal (language) more than the accompaniment, so S5 needs a language-free market descriptor (open item) before it can be controlled.
