CONTEXT (measured by us, not literature)
We study EEG during music listening -> valence/arousal (VA) -> VA-conditioned music generation (university project, non-commercial). On two open music-listening EEG datasets we ran subject-independent decoding (leave-one-subject-out, unseen songs, both) and found nothing above chance:
- MET (OpenNeuro ds008701): 20 subjects x 20 songs, 66 EEG channels, 120 s of music per trial, one rating of valence/arousal (1-9), liking, familiarity per trial, no audio in the release, no trigger channel (music onset only nominal; the README warns of playback/hardware delays). The IEEE DataPort copy reportedly lists only 8 channels.
- BCMI-MIdAS ds002722 (19 subjects, 32 channels, 40 s synthetic clips) and ds002724.
- Models tried: band-power ridge, handcrafted features, EEGNet-style CNN, frozen and fine-tuned CBraMod and LaBraM, covariance tangent-space features with per-subject re-centring. All trial-level CCC about 0 (95% subject-bootstrap CIs include 0). Random window splits give CCC up to 0.89 (leakage).
Diagnostics we measured on MET:
- Song explains 50% (valence) and 67% (arousal) of rating variance; split-half reliability of the 20 song means 0.96/0.98. A predictor that only knows the song gets r 0.67/0.79.
- EEG band power: subject explains 29% of variance, song 4%.
- Positive control fails: the song cannot be identified from EEG across subjects (accuracy 0.04-0.06, chance 0.05) with correlated component analysis (3 components), even with +-3 s lag tolerance, and with slow band-power envelope templates.
- Mass-univariate subject-centred band power vs rating: nothing significant after family-wise correction (except familiarity, p 0.035).
Our working interpretation (untested): the failure is upstream of emotion, i.e. EEG is not time-locked to the music at the precision needed (no trigger) or the music-driven signal is too weak, and ratings are largely song-determined.
