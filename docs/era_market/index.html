Which stem carries the era of a song, and can we regenerate it?

We study which stem, vocal or accompaniment, carries release-decade and release-market cues in Discogs-VI clips, then keep the original vocal and melody and regenerate the accompaniment in a requested decade. Read the report (PDF) or browse code, results and worklog.

Decade information is about as strong in the accompaniment as in the full mix; for market the vocal stem is as strong as the mix (MERT vocals vs accompaniment, Holm-adjusted p = .022). A second separator reproduces the ordering.

What the probes use

  • Stem swap. Vocal of one clip with the accompaniment of another clip of a different label: the decade prediction follows the accompaniment (34.8% vs 16.4% for the vocal), the market prediction follows the vocal (50.0% vs 18.3%).
  • Language. Erasing the sung-language subspace (rank 5) drops market accuracy from .588 to .353 for the mix; erasing five random directions leaves it at .591. For English-sung clips only, market accuracy is .397 (majority share .274).
  • Bandwidth. Band-limiting or adding hiss to real accompaniment moves the decade probes toward earlier decades; compression, clipping and a high shelf do not.
  • Labels are editions. In the full Discogs-VI metadata a composition has on average 2.42 release countries and 1.99 release decades.

Era-targeted covers

Original vocal kept; the accompaniment is regenerated by MusicGen-Melody from the chroma of the original mix and an era prompt. A low-rank adapter trained on decade-labelled accompaniment plus a three-branch guidance rule (melody term kept, era-contrast term added) moves the probes along the whole decade axis. Pick configurations below: the line is the expected decade the probe reports for clips made with each prompt (0 = 1960s). The dotted diagonal would be a perfect match.

  • With the adapter, the expected decade increases monotonically across the six prompts, and every change in target-decade probability (vs a neutral prompt) has a confidence interval that excludes 0. The unmodified model moves only the 1980s and 2010s; its 1970s prompt gives a lower target-decade probability than neutral.
  • The 1990s prompt is the weakest and depends on wording (a grunge wording gives the lowest effect, a eurodance wording the highest in MERT).
  • The song stays the song: song identification from the cover alone gives top-1 .875 at guidance 7 and 10 (neutral generations of the same adapter .944; chance .042).
  • An independent third encoder (Discogs-EffNet) orders the six prompts the same way. Against real clips of the prompted decade, FD-VGG is lower than for neutral generations for the 1980s and 2010s prompts (not for the 1960s), and Audiobox production quality does not drop at moderate guidance.

Weekly update, 9 November 2026: era and market as two controls

The goal is one control plane: a fixed song is regenerated as the point (release era, release market) moves. This week's results: the era axis is a continuous dose a (a = 0 is the 1960s prototype, a = 1 the 2010s prototype); the market axis is carried by the sung language (translated lyrics, vocal replaced by the target-language voice); both axes combine in one grid. All read-outs below are existing metrics; no new metric is proposed.

  • Between doses .3 and .7 the adapter is ahead of the control on MERT and EffNet in all three training seeds (dose .5: 3.25 vs 2.15 on MERT, three-seed means).
  • The distribution-level metric disagrees: FAD with sample-size extrapolation to the real 2010s does not rank the two models consistently across seeds, so we do not claim that the adapter generates more realistic audio of the decade; we claim it reaches the decade at a lower dose.
  • Beyond the end points the response saturates; a path 1960s → 1980s → 2010s increases gradually, with the 1990s and 2000s region reached by intermediate doses.

Market axis and the combined grid

  • The market move is carried by the sung language: erasing the language direction from the vocal probe removes it. Language share (0 to 100 % of code-switched lyrics) moves the Whisper language probability by +.71 to +.78 and the vocal market probe by +.28 (Spanish) and +.32 (German).
  • In the 5 × 5 grid (five era doses × five language shares, Spanish and German, 16 songs, three seeds) the era effect is +.62 to +1.00 expected decades at every language share and the language effect +.71 to +.78 at every era dose; the confidence intervals exclude zero.
  • Vocal-containing mixes are derived from commercial recordings and are not published here; the clips below are accompaniment only.

Evaluation protocol

Controllability: probe index and gain, R-Precision and multimodal distance in embedding space (adapted from Guo et al., CVPR 2022), dose monotonicity. Distribution: FAD per cell against real clips of the decade (MERT, MuQ), extrapolated to infinite sample size (Gui et al., arXiv 2311.01616). Variety: Diversity and MultiModality. Content: song identification, chroma similarity. Baselines are re-run on the same 24 songs; numbers from other papers are not mixed in. No listening test has been run.

Controls and limits

  • Prompts that name no decade but only bandwidth ("dull, band-limited, tape hiss") shift the probes by about half the effect of the 1960s prompt, so part of the era effect rides on bandwidth cues. Matching each clip's long-term spectrum to its neutral clip keeps the MERT 1980s and 2010s effects positive and halves the MuQ 2010s effect.
  • Nuisance removal: after erasing style and bandwidth directions from the probe, every adapter-plus-guidance effect stays positive but shrinks by .07 to .27; the unmodified model's 2010s effect disappears (MERT +.328 to -.020) while its 1980s effect stays (+.593 to +.608).
  • Language held fixed: for English-sung clips only, US-vs-UK market accuracy is .713 (chance .500) and the five-way task .398 (majority .281); numbers are pooled and not artist-grouped, so likely optimistic.
  • Release decade is a label of an edition, not of the recording; we could not recover Discogs ids, so splits are artist-disjoint but not guaranteed clique-disjoint.
  • 24 validation songs, 3 seeds; probes are proxies trained on the same real clips; no artist ids, test labels hidden; language and genre are proxies. No listening test has been run, so nothing here says how the covers sound to people.
